    01 · The data

    From COSO's Drive folder: three documents of three kinds

    The shared folder held eleven PDFs: engineering standards, a schedule of rates, a contract, two arbitration judgments and four Hindi documents. Three of them form one workflow a contractor really runs: read the tender, price the work, then live under the contract. Each is hard in a different way.

    CPWD contract 2020

    112 pages, born-digital English. Clause titles sit in the margin and come last in the text stream; the bilingual appendix uses a legacy Hindi font; Schedule F is blank in the template.

    344 passages, one clause part per page.

    RHB schedule of rates 2024

    824 pages of tables. A hierarchy printed flat, "-do-" ditto rows, item numbers that restart in every chapter, notes that change rates.

    9,649 passages: 8,159 rated rows, 1,158 parent groups, notes.

    Rajasthan PWD tender 68/2024-25

    6 pages, image-only Hindi scan. No text layer, handwritten fields, fee slabs, a conditional EMD of 0.5% or 2%.

    32 passages, each with an English twin.

    This live demo calls Bedrock only

    We could not deploy the fine-tuned model on this site. It runs on PyTorch, and with the embedder that means about 700 MB of model files and about 2 GB of memory per instance, beyond what this free hosting plan allows.     03 · Reading the scans

    How the images were read, and how we know they were read right

    1. Check every page's text layer"Has a text layer" is not "has usable text". Three cheap signals (orphan vowel signs, private-use characters, glued words) plus a legacy-font word list send bad pages to OCR: the corrupt PMGSY summary, 19 IS 456 pages, the contract's 17 Kruti Dev pages.
    2. Three independent readersClaude Sonnet 4.6, Gemma 3 27B and Llama 4 Maverick each transcribe the scan from the image.
    3. Vote on every numberNumbers are aligned across readings by their neighbouring words. Each disagreement goes to a stronger model with the page image and one targeted question: near "सांय __ बजे", is it 06.00 or 08.00?
    4. Translate, keeping every digitEach segment gets an English twin so English questions find Hindi text. A translation must contain exactly the original's numbers, or it is redone. Citations point at the Hindi page.

    The best single reader read the bid deadline 06.00 as 08.00 and a fee slab of 151 lakh as 161 lakh. Voting caught both; either would change a bid. Against 94 hand-read numeric fields the result is 94 of 94, and the EMDs reconcile: 2% and 0.5% of Rs 286.47 lakh are exactly 5,72,940 and 1,43,235.

    The printed tables in the rate book are read without OCR: generic table extractors recovered only 54% to 56% of hand-checked rows, so a positional parser anchors each row on its item number, rebuilds the parent chain across pages and expands ditto rows. An independently written parser agrees with it on 99.1% of 6,510 rates. When you click a citation, the server opens the PDF page with PyMuPDF and draws the stored box around the cited row.

    05 · Fine-tuning

    A cross-encoder fine-tuned to tell the right row from its neighbour

    The costliest failure in rates and contracts is a confident answer with a real citation to the wrong row: the Grading 2 rate taken from the wet-mix row, a 2.2 kW pump priced from another chapter. The number check passes, because the wrong number really is in the cited passage. A search model that embeds question and passage separately sees "20 mm" and a "15 mm" row as nearly identical. A cross-encoder reads them together and can learn that the size is the whole point.

    1. Questions from the corpusAn open model (gpt-oss-120b, Apache 2.0) wrote questions for 1,876 passages: specific, paraphrased, Hindi or Hinglish for English text, English for Hindi text.
    2. Near-missesFor the same passages, questions they do not answer, with one attribute changed: 30 mm to 25 mm, square metre to cubic metre, 4-pole to 3-pole.
    3. Hard negativesMined from the search itself. Sibling rows (same parent, different size) stay in as negatives, because telling them apart is the goal.
    4. No leakagePages were split into fixed blocks before any data existed (60% train, 20% development, 20% test). Training sees only train blocks, verified: zero held-out passages.
    5. Train on a laptop9,519 pairs, binary cross-entropy, word embeddings frozen, one epoch: 91 minutes on a 4-core CPU. The flag threshold (0.70) was chosen on development pages only.
    On held-out synthetic questions

    It learned the task

    Top-1 accuracy 0.537 with search alone, 0.652 with the cross-encoder off the shelf, 0.708 fine-tuned. Telling a real question from a one-attribute near-miss: AUROC 0.45 off the shelf (worse than chance), 0.84 fine-tuned.

    End to end, first try

    It made answers worse, and why

    Sorting by the fine-tuned score alone, with its threshold telling the writer "nothing answers", dropped accuracy from 83.9% to 80.7%. Real questions are longer than synthetic ones, so 48% of answerable ones scored under 0.70. The fix: fuse its order with the search order and show the score as a review flag. Result: 84.9% gold, 90.0% blind.

    Honest bottom line: fused into retrieval, the fine-tuned model ties the same model off the shelf on accuracy (86.1% each over 126 questions) and declines more of the questions that should be declined (16 of 19 against 11 of 19). A threshold learned on synthetic data is not a deployment threshold; it has to be calibrated on real, labelled questions.

    Against a hosted reranker: Cohere Rerank 3.5, a much larger commercial model, puts the right passage first more often on the e5 search (0.881 against 0.833). On the Cohere search used in this demo the 118M fine-tuned model is ahead on that measure (0.869 against 0.857) and behind on MRR and nDCG. A model trained in 91 minutes on a laptop stays close to it, and the GPU plan in section 06 is how to pass it. Every number is in section 08, Reranking tab.

    06 · Fine-tuning next

    How to take the fine-tuning further, with real data and real hardware

    The first round ran on synthetic questions and a laptop CPU. With real users, domain experts and GPUs, the plan has six steps. Each step must beat the current system on the gold and blind sets, per document and per question type, before it ships.

    1. Collect real questionsLog every question with its passages, scores and verifier result. Answers with a low score or a verifier warning go to a review queue, where a quantity surveyor and a contracts manager mark the right passage and the right answer. A few hundred labelled questions a month is enough to start.
    2. Calibrate before trusting the scoreFit the review-flag threshold on those real labels (isotonic or temperature scaling) and track calibration error. Only a calibrated score may decline an answer on its own.
    3. A bigger reranker, trained on GPUsMove from a 118M model to a larger multilingual cross-encoder (around 0.5B parameters, the bge-reranker-v2-m3 class), several epochs, a learning-rate sweep, negatives refreshed from production. Distil from a strong judge: let Claude score candidate passages and train the reranker to match. One A10G- or L4-class GPU handles this in hours, not days.
    4. Fine-tune the search model tooTrain the embedder on the same real pairs with in-batch and mined negatives, then retrain the reranker on its output, so the two improvements add up instead of overlapping.
    5. Adapt an open answer writerSupervised fine-tuning with LoRA on an open model (gpt-oss-20b or Qwen3 32B) using answers that passed the verifier, the judge and an expert; then preference tuning (DPO) on pairs where one answer declines a near-miss correctly and the other does not. This keeps data in India on self-hosted GPUs and cuts cost per question.
    6. Teach a vision model the scansFine-tune an open vision-language model on Devanagari tenders with boxes, so scanned citations get highlights too and the pipeline no longer needs a closed model as its primary reader.

    07 · Evaluation

    How it was measured

    A 96-question development set (32 contract, 36 rates, 28 tender) written from the pages, covering lookups, interpretation, exact rates, child and ditto rows, notes, Hindi and Hinglish, conditional amounts, and questions that should be declined: unanswerable, near-miss and false-premise. A 30-question blind set written after the code freeze, never read while building, run once per configuration. A judge from a different model family grades correctness against the gold answer; a deterministic key-fact check gives a lower bound.

    08 · All metrics

    Every stage, every configuration, every standard metric

    Computed by evals/all_metrics.py from the evaluation runs in the repository. Retrieval and reranking use the 84 answerable gold questions; a passage counts as relevant when it carries the gold clause or rate item, or sits on the gold tender page. The best value in each column is marked.

    Loading the metrics.

    What each metric means

    Hit@kAt least one relevant passage is in the top k. Hit@8 is what the answer writer gets to see.
    Recall@kShare of the question's gold references (clauses, rows, pages) found in the top k.
    Precision@kShare of the top k passages that are relevant. Low by design: a question usually has one or two right passages.
    MRR@10Mean of 1 / rank of the first relevant passage; 1.0 means it is always first.
    nDCG@10Rewards relevant passages near the top, one gain per gold reference, normalised so a perfect ranking scores 1.
    Page Hit@5The right page is in the top 5, even if the exact clause or row is not.
    AUROCHow well a score separates two groups (real vs near-miss questions); 0.5 is chance, 1.0 perfect.
    Judged correctA Claude judge compares the answer with the gold answer; 95% bootstrap interval below the value.
    Key-fact recallShare of the gold key facts found in the answer by plain matching (no model involved).
    All key factsEvery key fact found: a strict, deterministic lower bound on correctness.
    Citation hitAt least one citation points at the gold clause, rate row or tender page.
    Citation precisionShare of an answer's citations that point at gold passages.
    Negatives handledUnanswerable, near-miss and false-premise questions that the answer declined or corrected.
    Refusal precisionOf the answers that declined, the share that should have.
    Over-refusalAnswerable questions that were declined. Lower is better.
    Unsupported numberAnswers containing a number that is neither in a cited source nor one arithmetic step away. Lower is better.
    Quotes verifiedAnswers whose every quote was found in the source it was attributed to.
    CERCharacter error rate of OCR against a hand transcription. Lower is better.
    09 · Limits

    What still breaks, and what comes next

    • Rate notes are separate passages. "Add 5% for grade AA pipes" is applied only when retrieval brings the note along. Next: attach each note's scope to the items it modifies.
    • About 3% of rate rows (unnumbered variants, rate ranges, multi-column hire charges) sit in one record with several rates.
    • Scanned citations have no box, because vision models return text without coordinates.
    • Schedule F is blank in this contract template, so "who decides" answers point to it. A real deployment ingests each contract's own Schedule F.
    • The review flag is uncalibrated and flags about half of real, correct answers. It routes to review; it never blocks.
    • Small test sets (96 + 30 questions) give intervals of roughly ±8 and ±15 points.
    • The guardrail was tested on 56 messages I wrote, and its prompt was changed once after the first run. Real traffic will bring messages it has not seen; it searches whenever it is unsure, and logged routes should be reviewed like answers.
    • At scale: per-page ingestion keyed by content hash, Postgres with pgvector and keyword search filtered by project and version, effective dates on every passage, the test sets as a deploy gate, and a review queue fed by low scores.
